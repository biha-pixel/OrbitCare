  1. An astronaut’s readings change. OrbitCare makes the personal comparison and review reasoning inspectable.
  2. Fixed mission days 1–14 establish a personal reference. The selected reading is shown beside it in original units.
  3. On synthetic day 22, the first changed reading has not yet met the three-day review rule.
  4. An actual Mission Decision Replay interaction steps through the reference, changed readings, persistence and review flag. Earlier states hide future readings and events.
  5. NASA cardiovascular adaptation, sleep, longitudinal research and medical-care resources inform indicator context and review boundaries. They do not validate the prototype thresholds.
  6. The separate OSD-575 view preserves 56 original sodium/potassium observations from four participants and seven ground visits in mmol/L. These measurements do not calibrate the monitoring rules.
  7. The browser is disconnected and the prepared app reloads offline. A local review is recorded and survives another offline reload. Nothing is sent to medical support.
  8. OrbitCare is a research prototype, not diagnosis, treatment or a flight-certified medical system. AI-assisted development and synthetic fixtures are disclosed.
